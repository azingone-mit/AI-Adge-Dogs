# Two-minute video script: Kennel Optimizer

Audience: a Malaysian shelter manager. About 290 words, roughly 2 minutes spoken. Screen directions are in brackets.

**[0:00 to 0:15] Hook.** [Photo of a full kennel row, then the cover slide]
In Malaysia, a shelter dog waits about 52 days to be adopted, and more than a quarter of listed animals are still waiting after 100 days. Every one of those days costs food and care. It also blocks a space that another rescue needs.

**[0:15 to 0:35] The decision.** [Problem slide]
Shelter managers spend a small budget on vet care, photos and fee discounts, but nobody tells them which animals need help most, or which fixes actually work.

**[0:35 to 1:05] The AI.** [Solution slide, then photo chart]
Kennel Optimizer reads each listing: the animal’s profile, its description, and its photo, using an AI vision model. It predicts how many days each animal will wait. Then it tests every affordable fix, prices it with a live web search, and picks the best plan for your budget.
One surprise: the AI found that a well-lit, close-up portrait cuts about nine days, while simple pixel sharpness doesn’t help at all.

**[1:05 to 1:35] Does it work?** [Benchmark chart, then budget curve]
We tested it on 50 real Malaysian rescuers it had never seen. Free fixes alone, like five phone photos, a short video and an AI-written bio, saved over 13,000 animal-days. That is room for about 287 more rescues. With money on top, the optimizer saved six times more days than spending on the cheapest actions first. Spending on vet care first barely moved adoption speed.

**[1:35 to 1:55] Demo.** [Demo slide, highlight Sparkles row]
For one Penang rescuer with 171 animals, the plan is free fixes this week, plus three photo sessions for the 45 listings that gain most. Total saved: 911 animal-days.

**[1:55 to 2:00] Ask.** [Recommendations slide]
Give us one shelter and one month, and we’ll prove it.
